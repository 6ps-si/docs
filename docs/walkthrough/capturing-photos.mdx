A walkthrough is built from photos of your rooms. This page explains what kind of photo to take, how the editor treats it, and how to add it.

## 360° cameras

Studio supports 360° cameras such as Insta360, so you can bring photos from the camera into the editor and build a tour without other software. A phone works too, since the whole flow runs in the browser.

## Image requirements

The editor accepts JPG, PNG, and WebP files.

For a **360° Image**, use an equirectangular photo. This is the flat, wide format that 360° cameras export, where the full sphere is unrolled into a rectangle twice as wide as it is tall.

When you publish, Studio prepares each 360° photo in your browser:

- It scales the photo down to a maximum of 4096 pixels on its longest side and about 10 MB before processing.
- It cuts the photo into six cube faces, each 2048 pixels, that the viewer loads.
- It makes smaller, lightweight versions so visitors see the room quickly while the full-quality version loads.

You do not need to resize your photos yourself. A photo larger than 4096 pixels is scaled down, so shoot at the resolution your camera gives you and let Studio handle the rest.

## Adding a photo

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the file">
    Drag an image onto the drop zone that says **Drag & drop a 360° or flat image**, or click it to browse. Once you have a scene, use **Add** in the **Scenes** list, or **Add room** inside a scene.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say what type it is">
    When you drop or browse for a file in the drop zone, a dialog asks **What type of image is this?** Choose **360° Image** or **Flat Image**. The **Add** and **Add room** menus already let you choose the type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the room">
    The photo appears in the viewer and as a room card marked **360°** or **FLAT**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If no scene is selected, Studio creates one for you, named **Scene** followed by a number, and adds the photo as its first room.

To swap a photo later, use the upload icon on the room card. To remove one, use the trash icon.

## Flat photos

A **Flat Image** is a regular photo shown as an image, without the look-around 360° view. Use one when a 360° photo is not the right fit, such as for an aerial photo of a property, a floor plan, or a detail shot. A flat image can hold buttons, so it can link into 360° rooms. See [Building a tour](/docs/walkthrough/building-a-tour).

## Shooting tips

- Take one 360° photo for each room, from a spot that shows the room well.
- Keep the lighting consistent from room to room so the tour feels continuous.
- Keep the camera steady and at a natural height.

## Removing the tripod

If your camera or tripod shows up in a 360° photo, you can remove it. The AI room editing tool paints out unwanted objects inside the walkthrough editor, with no separate photo-editing software. See [AI room editing](/docs/walkthrough/ai-room-editing).
