A camera position is a saved view of your model: where the camera looks, how far away it is, and how far a visitor can turn it. You build a list of positions in the **Positions** panel and fine-tune each one in **Position Settings**.

## Saving a view

<Steps>
  <Step title="Frame the model">
    Orbit, pan, and zoom in the viewer until the model looks the way you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a position">
    Click the **+** button in the **Positions** panel. Studio saves the current view as a new position named **New Position** followed by a number, and opens its settings on the right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refine it">
    Rename it, add a description, and adjust the limits as described below.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Click a position in the list to move the camera to it and open its settings. To change a saved view later, frame the model again, select the position, and click **Capture** in **Position Settings**. Capture replaces the position's angle, zoom, and target with the current view and leaves its limits as they are.

## Naming and describing

In **Position Settings**, the **Name** field sets the label shown in the position list. The **Description** field is a rich-text editor. Its content appears to viewers when the position is active, so use it to explain what they are looking at.

## Movement between positions

**Animation Duration** sets how many seconds the camera takes to travel to this position. The default is 2 seconds and the shortest value is 0.1 seconds. Type a number, use the arrows, or scroll the mouse wheel over the field.

## Framing and limits

Three sliders control how the view looks and how much freedom a visitor has. Each one has a handle for the current value and handles for the minimum and maximum.

| Slider | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| **Rotation X** | The left and right camera angle, and how far a visitor can turn it. |
| **Rotation Y** | The up and down camera angle, and how far a visitor can turn it. |
| **Zoom** | The default zoom, plus the closest and farthest zoom a visitor can reach. |

Drag a limit handle to set a boundary. On the two rotation sliders, dragging a limit handle all the way to the edge switches that limit off, so a visitor can turn freely in that direction.

Tight limits keep visitors on the views you designed. Wide limits let them explore.

<Tip>
  The overall lens angle of the camera is a scene-wide setting. See [Scene and environment](/docs/editor/scene-and-environment).
</Tip>

## Target

**Target** is the point in the scene the camera orbits around, entered as X, Y, and Z values. Frame the view and click **Capture** to fill these in for you, or type values to fine-tune them.

## Ordering

The first position in the list is the primary position, the view your model opens with.

- Drag the grip handle at the left of a position to change its order.
- Open the menu next to a position and choose **Primary Position** to move it to the top of the list.
- Open the menu and choose **Delete** to remove a position. The last remaining position cannot be deleted.
