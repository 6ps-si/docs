The 3D Editor runs in your browser. You load a model, decide how it looks and behaves for viewers, and publish it as a link or an embed. No code is involved.

The editor handles standard mesh models and 3D Gaussian Splatting files. That covers large building scenes as well as single-object scans for product pages. See [Supported formats](/docs/reference/supported-formats) for the full list.

Viewers explore a published model live in their browser. The aim is to shorten the wait for something you can show a client from about an hour of rendering to roughly ten seconds.

## The layout

On a desktop the editor has three areas.

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="Left sidebar" icon="sidebar">
    A back arrow to your library, then the **Objects**, **Positions**, and **Buttons** panels, and an **Advanced** panel for visual effects.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Viewer" icon="cube">
    Your model in the middle. Before you add a model, this area is a drop zone that says **Drag and drop your 3D asset here**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Right sidebar" icon="sliders">
    **Scene info** at the top, followed by settings for the selected position, button, scene, and model, and the **AI Render** panel.
  </Card>
</Columns>

- **Objects** lists the models in your scene. Select one to edit its settings. See [Uploading models](/docs/editor/uploading-models) and [Model settings](/docs/editor/model-settings).
- **Positions** holds the saved camera views. See [Camera positions](/docs/editor/camera-positions).
- **Buttons** places clickable points in the scene. See [Hotspots](/docs/editor/hotspots).
- **Scene Settings** covers the background, lighting, and other scene-wide options. See [Scene and environment](/docs/editor/scene-and-environment).
- **AI Render** creates an AI-edited image from the current view. See [AI render](/docs/editor/ai-render).
- **Scene info** holds the name, description, visibility, and the **Publish** button. See [Publishing](/docs/editor/publishing) and [Visibility and ownership](/docs/library/visibility-and-ownership).

Below the editor, the **Statistics** shortcut in the right sidebar scrolls to a section with the view and engagement figures for the model. See [Engagement and analytics](/docs/sharing/engagement-and-analytics).

## Trying it without an account

You can open the editor and try its features without signing in. You need an account to publish. When you click **Publish** while signed out, a sign-in window opens, and you can carry on once you are signed in. See [Accounts](/docs/get-started/accounts).
